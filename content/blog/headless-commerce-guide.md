In a traditional e-commerce platform, the storefront customers see and the commerce engine that manages products, carts and orders are tightly coupled. In a **headless** architecture, they are separated: the commerce engine exposes APIs, and a custom frontend — often built with a framework like Next.js — consumes them.

The result can be dramatically faster pages, complete design freedom and the ability to sell through many channels from one back end. But headless is not automatically the right choice.

## The benefits of going headless

### Performance

Custom frontends can be optimised aggressively: pre-rendered pages, edge caching, image optimisation and minimal JavaScript. Faster pages convert better, and every 100 milliseconds can matter.

### Experience freedom

Brands with distinctive identities, rich content or unusual buying journeys are no longer constrained by theme templates.

### Omnichannel

The same commerce APIs can power a website, a mobile app, in-store kiosks, marketplaces and even voice or chat interfaces.

### Composability

You can choose best-in-class services for search, content, reviews and personalisation, and swap them over time.

## The trade-offs

Headless introduces real costs:

- **More engineering.** You are now building and maintaining a frontend application, not configuring a theme.
- **More integrations.** Features that came "for free" in a monolithic platform — previews, app extensions, some checkout features — may need custom work.
- **More moving parts.** Hosting, caching, monitoring and deployment pipelines all need care.
- **Different skills.** Marketing teams need a good content management experience, which must be designed deliberately.

## A simple decision framework

Headless is usually a strong fit when you have:

- Significant online revenue where performance gains translate into meaningful money.
- A brand experience that templates cannot deliver.
- Multiple regions, languages, currencies or channels.
- An in-house or partner engineering team to support it long term.

A modern hosted platform with a well-built theme is usually the better choice when:

- You are launching or still finding product-market fit.
- Your catalogue and journeys are relatively standard.
- Speed to market and low running costs matter more than differentiation.

> Choose the simplest architecture that will still serve you well in three years.

## A middle path

Many brands begin with a hosted platform and adopt headless gradually — for example, a headless content-rich landing experience while checkout remains on the platform. This "progressive headless" approach captures many of the benefits at lower risk.

## Getting the migration right

Whether you go headless or replatform to a modern hosted solution, protect what you have built:

1. **Preserve SEO** with a complete URL map, redirects and structured data.
2. **Migrate data carefully** — products, customers, orders and reviews.
3. **Test checkout exhaustively** across payment methods, currencies and devices.
4. **Measure before and after** — conversion rate, average order value and page speed.
